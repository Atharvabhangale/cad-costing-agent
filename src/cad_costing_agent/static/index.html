      How setups and times are charged

      What actually gets costed, grouped into setups. A setup is one fixturing of the part: its setup time is charged once, however many operations it holds, while each operation is charged for its own cycle time. Rates come from the rate set; you enter only times, and operation quantity always matches the part quantity. An estimated time is a proposal, not a commitment — a row is costed only once you accept its estimate or type your own, and whatever you change, what the system suggested is kept beside it.

          Component classification

          No CAD format states make/buy. The system suggests manufactured wherever the file carries measurable solid geometry — that is evidence about what can be costed here, not evidence that the part is made rather than bought. A purchased bearing is modelled exactly like a machined housing. Confirm each component or change it; both the suggestion and your answer are kept.

          Component manufacturing plans

          Every manufactured component is planned on its own: its own features, its own operations, its own setups. A setup is one fixturing of one part, so two components never share one however alike their processes look, and there is no assembly-level routing here — nothing in this application knows what order components are made or joined in. A purchased, standard or excluded component has no machining plan, which is the answer rather than a gap.

          Component cost roll-up

          A manufactured component is costed as a part by the same engine as anything else. A purchased or standard one contributes the unit cost you entered. An excluded one contributes nothing, on purpose. Each is then multiplied by how many times this assembly uses it.

          How many assemblies you are building. This is not a component's occurrence count: a bearing used 4 times in an assembly built 10 times is 40 bearings, and the two numbers are multiplied exactly once, on the server.

          Quotation

          A quotation presents a stored estimate as a printable document. It recalculates nothing: the figures are read from the snapshot taken when the estimate was created, so it keeps saying what that estimate said however the rates change later.

          Applied on top of material and operation cost. Overhead and scrap are percentages of material + operations only, so they never compound on each other or on tooling. Tooling is a one-time charge for the whole batch.

          Time-assumption profiles

          A time profile holds the machining assumptions an estimated time is worked out from — feeds, speeds, approach allowances, depths of cut. It holds no money. Rates decide what an hour costs; these decide how long the work takes, and the two are versioned separately because a shop revises them on different schedules. A published version is never edited: changing an assumption means publishing a new version, and every estimate already made keeps the version that produced it.

          Every parameter below is used verbatim by the deterministic time model — nothing is scaled or second-guessed. The spindle limits are the one exception, and they clamp rather than reject, because a machine really does have a top and bottom speed.

          Drilling

          A hole larger than the threshold is drilled smallest first: the pilot, then drills each at most the ratio times the previous, then the hole's own size — with the defaults, Ø20 is drilled 6 → 15 → 20. Illustrative values from common practice; no standard prescribes them.
